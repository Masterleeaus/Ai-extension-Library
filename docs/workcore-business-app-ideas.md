# WorkCore Field and Home Service Business App Portfolio

## Purpose

This document converts the reusable engines discovered inside the extension archives into **complete standalone business applications** for field and home service businesses.

A concept only qualifies as an application when it owns a durable system of record, delivers an end-to-end business outcome, has clear roles and permissions, can be sold independently, integrates with WorkCore without duplicating authority, and has a credible pricing model.

Ideas such as Quote Rescue, RouteFill, ScopeLock, Parts Hunter, Crew Shadow and Outcome Mirror are treated as **features or modules**, not standalone products.

## Portfolio principles

- WorkCore remains the operational system of record for customers, properties, jobs, staff, invoices and service history.
- Each app owns a specialised domain and exposes events and APIs to WorkCore.
- BYO AI and provider keys should remain available without platform markup.
- Titan-managed usage should be transparent and capped.
- Tenant ownership, encrypted credentials, verified webhooks, audit history and durable queues are mandatory.
- AI output must expose uncertainty where incorrect decisions could affect safety, pricing, compliance or customer rights.

# Shared engines to extract

The portfolio should reuse common engines rather than copying code between applications.

1. **Vision Grounding Engine** — assets, defects, surfaces, hazards, completed work, bounding boxes, masks and polygons.
2. **Consensus Engine** — independent assessments, agreement, disagreement, confidence and evidence sufficiency.
3. **Evidence Engine** — capture metadata, chronology, provenance, retention, signed reports and controlled sharing.
4. **Spatial Document Engine** — Tiptap and Konva documents, site maps, visual scopes, work instructions and proposal boards.
5. **Commercial Rules Engine** — pricing, eligibility, discounts, approvals, effective dates and usage limits.
6. **Geospatial Action Engine** — discovery, territory analysis, suppliers, providers and WorkCore promotion.
7. **Communications Engine** — phone, email, SMS, WhatsApp, structured replies, consent and escalation.
8. **Media Project Engine** — timeline editing, captions, dubbing, voice isolation, rendering and asynchronous provider jobs.
9. **Migration Engine** — connectors, schema mapping, transformation, validation, reconciliation and cutover.
10. **Workflow Runtime** — durable events, queues, retries, idempotency, approvals and dead-letter handling.

# Productisation risks found in the donor code

- Very limited automated test coverage.
- Request authorisation that frequently defaults to true.
- Controllers that use raw IDs without reliable tenant ownership checks.
- Credentials and message payloads stored or logged without a shared encrypted vault and redaction layer.
- Generic table names that may collide across modules.
- Direct coupling to host helpers, MagicAI credits and global authentication assumptions.
- Inconsistent queueing, polling, retry and failure handling.
- Weak or missing webhook signature verification in several channel integrations.
- Duplicated scheduling, publishing and analytics implementations.
- Missing consistent domain events between apps.

# Continuing documentation workflow

For every future archive or idea-generation pass:

1. Inspect source code rather than relying on extension names or sales descriptions.
2. Extract reusable technical engines and identify concrete donor components.
3. Separate infrastructure, features, modules and complete applications.
4. Reject any standalone app that lacks a durable central record and end-to-end workflow.
5. Check new concepts against this portfolio to prevent duplication.
6. Define positioning, users, records, workflow, modules, WorkCore integration, security, analytics and pricing.
7. State required new engineering and donor-code reuse honestly.
8. Save each themed result under `workcore/` as Markdown.
9. Use lowercase kebab-case file names.
10. Update the `workcore/README.md` index.

## Quality gate

A concept is ready to add only when these questions are answered:

- What durable business record does the app own?
- What complete customer outcome does it deliver?
- Why is it not merely a feature of another application?
- Which field or home service verticals benefit?
- Which donor engines can be reused?
- What genuinely new domain engineering is required?
- How does it integrate with WorkCore without duplicating authority?
- What are the security, privacy and tenancy requirements?
- How is business value measured?
- What is the AUD pricing model?
- What creates a defensible moat after visible features are copied?

## Document status vocabulary

- **Validated** — supported by code evidence and commercial rationale.
- **Prioritised** — accepted into the product roadmap.
- **In development** — implementation has begun.
- **Released** — usable production version exists.
